ISSUES:  
1. **MAJOR – Pre‑registration breach:** The confirmatory hypotheses H1b and H3 were intended to include *all* seven models. The manuscript excludes deepseek‑v4 from both analyses because its judge‑task outputs were empty, a deviation that was not stipulated in the frozen protocol and therefore demotes the claims to exploratory.  
2. **MAJOR – Statistical over‑interpretation:** The “strong support” claim for H1 rests on a between/within distance ratio of 2.45 and a silhouette mean of 0.288 (95 % CI [0.155, 0.416]), which indicates only modest clustering. Treating the permutation p‑value (p ≈ 10⁻⁴) as proof of a large effect, and limiting the analysis to the top‑2 MCA dimensions that capture only ~15 % of inertia, constitute misuse of effect‑size reporting.  
3. **MAJOR – Over‑claiming habitus:** The manuscript equates statistical session stability with Bourdieu’s habitus—a sociological construct that entails durable, structuring dispositions across fields. Demonstrating clustering in a low‑dimensional space does not substantiate the deeper theoretical claims about structured structures or transposability.  
4. **MINOR – Confounding variables:** All items are in Japanese, so language effects are confounded with model architecture. Quantized local models (Gemma) and API‑based models (Claude, DeepSeek) differ not only in size but also in inference backend, yet the analysis treats them as comparable, leaving open alternative explanations for observed clusters.  
5. **MINOR – Reproducibility gaps:** No a‑priori power analysis is provided despite the small sample (35 sessions). Random‑seed generation for permutation tests and the exact versions of backend APIs are not fully documented, limiting independent replication even though code and data are released.
